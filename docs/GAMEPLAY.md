# Gameplay guide

## Premise

You arrive by bus in a small town to take over the house of your late uncle.
He left you a letter: he did not believe his heart gave out, and he was sure
one of the neighbours goes out at night in a hooded coat and kills. The letter
contains one true detail about the killer.

The town lives on without you. Every resident has a home, a household, a job
with shifts, a daily routine, needs, a personality, relationships and
memories. One of them is the **Night Neighbour**. Usually another one is a
burglar. The police patrol, respond to calls and investigate on their own.

## Goal and endings

You have a fixed number of nights (3, 5 or 7, chosen when starting).

| Ending | How | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Night Neighbour is caught** | You accuse the right person to the police chief with enough evidence, and officers arrest them | Win |
| **The police got there first** | Officers arrest the killer on their own (witness reports, scene evidence) | Win |
| **The Night Neighbour is dead** | You kill the killer and the evidence (hood seen, journal fit, self-defence) backs you | Win |
| **Murderer** | You kill the killer with nothing to show for it | Loss |
| **Arrested for murder** | Witnesses identified you killing someone and you are taken into custody | Loss |
| **You did not survive** | Your health reaches zero | Loss |
| **The Night Neighbour got away** | The morning after the last night arrives with the killer free | Loss |

Every ending screen offers *New game (same town)*, *New game (new town)*,
*Load autosave* (after a loss) and *Main menu*.

## Progression

The objectives panel (top left, and the journal) guides the first day and then
follows the investigation:

1. **Find your uncle's house** – follow the marker on the compass. You have
   the key.
2. **Read the letter** on the table.
3. **Buy a flashlight** at the hardware store, and **introduce yourself** to
   neighbours – residents you have met are named in the journal and tell you
   more.
4. **Lock your doors** in the evening (hold E at a door).
5. **Survive the night.** The killer hunts at night; the director escalates:
   the hunt starts at a different time each night, from the fourth night (or
   after a night without a kill) two victims are possible, and once the killer
   considers you a threat (you have been asking the right questions, collecting
   clues or watching them) they will come for you – trying your door handle,
   knocking, breaking a window.
6. After a murder: **examine the scene**, talk to the **police chief**, find
   out what happened to **missing** residents, and **gather evidence**.
7. With three or more clues, **accuse** the culprit to the chief.

Each morning the game autosaves.

## Investigation

* **Physical evidence** – the killer leaves traces at scenes: coat fibres,
  footprints (shoe type and height), hair, cigarette butts, sometimes the
  weapon. The lair (a shed or abandoned house) holds stolen keepsakes. Pick
  evidence up to add it to the journal.
* **Testimony** – ask residents what they saw. Answers come from the NPC's
  actual memories and say how they know: *"I saw…"* (witnessed), *"I heard…"*
  (heard only), *"X told me…"* (hearsay, less reliable). Ask where they were
  last night for alibis, who wears a particular coat, who smokes.
* **Your own sightings** – watching residents teaches you their height, coat,
  shoes, hair and whether they smoke. Seeing a hooded figure at night, or
  somebody leaving home at 3 am, is recorded.
* **Suspects page** – every known resident is compared with the clues and
  each trait is tagged as a match, a conflict or still unknown.
* **Accusing** – the chief weighs the evidence that fits the accused
  (≥ 3.0 strength with at most one conflict). A rejected accusation costs
  nothing but time; an accepted accusation of an innocent gets them arrested
  and, when the killing continues, released – and you lose the chief's trust.
  With no trust left the chief stops listening.

## Survival systems

* **Health** – regenerates slowly while fed. Bleeding drains it until you use
  a bandage; painkillers heal over time; the clinic's doctor patches you up.
* **Stamina** – sprinting, dodging, swinging and blocking use it.
* **Hunger / energy** – eat food, sleep in a bed (until morning or a 2-hour
  nap). Loud noises nearby wake you. Waiting (T) passes time while awake.
* **Stealth** – the eye icon shows how visible you are (light at your
  position, crouching, flashlight), the noise meter how loud you are. A lit
  flashlight shows you the way and shows you to everybody else.
* **Money** – buy food, medicine, batteries, planks, lockpicks and weapons at
  shops; sell valuables (watches, jewellery, silverware) to shopkeepers.
* **Defending your home** – lock doors, board windows from inside with planks,
  switch room lights off so you are harder to see from outside (E while
  aiming at nothing toggles the room light), hide in wardrobes.

## Combat

Melee only. Weapons: fists, kitchen knife (fast, makes victims bleed), baseball
bat (slow, heavy, knocks down), crowbar, police baton.
Left mouse attacks with a wind-up and cooldown, right mouse blocks (reduces
damage when facing the attacker), Space dodges. Hits are resolved against
actual geometry: you cannot hit through walls or closed doors, but swings
break windows and batter doors. Wounds bleed and leave blood, a downed person
can get up again, and a dead one stays where they fell.

NPCs react: most flee and call the police; brave ones fight back; the killer
fights hard. **Violence is witnessed.** Anybody who sees you attack someone
remembers it; if they know you they can name you to the police, who will come
to arrest you. Comply and you spend a night in the cells with a fine (unless
you killed someone in front of witnesses), resist and you run.

## Controls

All keys can be rebound in *Settings → Controls*.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W A S D, mouse | Move, look (click the view to capture the mouse) |
| Arrow keys | Look without a mouse |
| Shift | Sprint |
| C | Crouch / sneak (toggle) |
| E | Interact – doors, items, furniture, people. **Hold** for alternatives: lock/unlock, pick locks, board windows, drag bodies, search wardrobes |
| Left mouse | Attack |
| Right mouse | Block |
| Space | Dodge |
| F | Flashlight |
| 1–6, wheel, H | Select weapon slot, cycle, holster |
| Tab | Inventory (use, equip, drop) |
| J / M | Journal / map |
| T | Wait |
| Esc | Pause menu (save, load, settings, observer, quit) |
| F2 | Observer mode |

## Observer mode

Available from the main menu (a town without the player) and from the pause
menu or F2 during a game (the live simulation of your current game). Fly with
WASD/Space/C (Shift faster), set time speed (pause, 1×, 4×, 16×, 60×), toggle
path, view-cone and navigation overlays, and click any resident to follow and
inspect them: needs, current activity and task queue, destination, traits,
appearance and their most recent memories with source. The panel also lists
recent incidents and police cases. The roles of every resident are shown,
so observer mode spoils the mystery.

## Settings

Graphics quality (low/medium/high: resolution scale, sun and flashlight
shadows and their resolution, number of real-time point lights, texture
filtering, anti-aliasing), post-processing, brightness, field of view, head
bob, mouse sensitivity and invert, master/music/effects/ambience volume, HRTF
spatial audio, FPS counter, and key bindings. Settings are stored in the
browser.
